## Demolition Does Not Have to Be Stressful

Whether you are tearing down an old deck, removing a backyard shed, or gutting a bathroom for renovation, demolition projects can feel overwhelming. But with a bit of preparation, the process is smooth, safe, and surprisingly fast.

Here is a step-by-step guide to help you prepare for a demolition project at your home.

## 1. Define the Scope of Your Project

Before anything else, get clear on exactly what needs to come down. Walk the area with your contractor or demolition team and discuss:

- What structures or materials are being removed
- What is staying (walls, flooring, fixtures you want to keep)
- Where debris will be staged and loaded
- Any underground utilities, pipes, or wiring in the area

The more specific you are upfront, the fewer surprises there will be on demo day.

## 2. Check if You Need a Permit

In Ontario, certain demolition work requires a permit from your local municipality. Generally:

- **Deck removal** — usually does not require a permit
- **Shed demolition** — typically does not require a permit for small structures
- **Fence removal** — no permit needed
- **Interior demolition** — may require a permit depending on the scope, especially if you are removing load-bearing walls
- **Garage or large structure demolition** — almost always requires a permit

Check with your municipal building department. If you are in London, Lambton Shores, or any of our service areas, we can help you navigate the permit process.

## 3. Disconnect Utilities

If the structure being demolished is connected to electricity, water, gas, or plumbing, these must be disconnected before work begins. This is critical for safety.

- **Electricity** — have an electrician disconnect and cap any wiring
- **Gas** — contact your gas provider to shut off and disconnect the line
- **Water/plumbing** — shut off and cap any water lines running to the structure

Never attempt demolition on a structure with live utilities.

## 4. Clear the Area

Remove anything you want to keep from the demolition zone and the surrounding area. This includes:

- Patio furniture, planters, and decor
- Vehicles, trailers, and equipment
- Valuables stored in or near the structure
- Garden hoses, outdoor lighting, and irrigation systems

Also clear a path for the demolition crew to access the site and stage their equipment and trailer.

## 5. Protect Your Landscaping

Demolition can be rough on nearby landscaping. If you have gardens, trees, or features you want to protect:

- Lay plywood sheets over garden beds near the work zone
- Mark sprinkler heads and irrigation lines with flags
- Discuss access routes with your demolition crew to avoid driving over lawn areas
- Consider temporary fencing around delicate plantings

A good demolition crew will work carefully around your property, but it helps to flag what matters most to you.

## 6. Talk to Your Neighbours

Demolition is loud. Give your neighbours a heads-up about the project, especially the timing. Let them know it might be noisy for a day or two but that you have hired professionals who will keep the site clean and contained.

This small courtesy goes a long way and prevents complaints.

## 7. Understand What Happens to the Debris

Ask your demolition team about their disposal process. At Complete Junk Removal, we sort all demolition debris to recycle as much material as possible:

- **Wood** — recycled or repurposed when possible
- **Metal** — taken to scrap recycling
- **Concrete and masonry** — recycled at aggregate facilities
- **Mixed debris** — disposed of responsibly at licensed facilities

Everything is included in our quote — demolition, loading, hauling, and disposal.

## 8. Plan for After the Demolition

Once the structure is gone, you will be left with a cleared area. Think ahead about:

- Do you need the area graded or leveled?
- Are you building something new in its place?
- Do you need topsoil or seed for grass restoration?

We leave the site clean and debris-free, but grading and landscaping are typically a separate step.
