## Why Spring Is the Best Time to Declutter

After a long Ontario winter spent mostly indoors, spring is the natural time to open the windows, let the fresh air in, and deal with all the stuff that has been piling up. A proper spring cleanout is not just about tidying — it is about reclaiming your space and starting the season with a clean slate.

Whether you are doing a light declutter or a full-home purge, this guide will walk you through it room by room.

## Room-by-Room Cleanout Checklist

### Kitchen

The kitchen accumulates clutter faster than almost any room. Go through:

- **Small appliances** you never use (bread maker, fondue set, extra blender)
- **Expired pantry items** and spices
- **Chipped or mismatched dishes** and glasses
- **Worn-out cookware** — scratched non-stick pans, dented pots
- **Plastic containers** without matching lids
- **Old cleaning supplies** under the sink

### Living Room and Family Room

- **Old electronics** — VCRs, DVD players, outdated game consoles
- **Books and magazines** you have already read
- **Worn-out throw pillows and blankets**
- **Broken or unused furniture** — that recliner nobody sits in
- **Kids toys** that have been outgrown

### Bedrooms

- **Clothing** that does not fit or has not been worn in a year
- **Old mattresses and box springs** — if it is over 8 years old, it is time
- **Broken furniture** — wobbly nightstands, damaged dressers
- **Extra bedding and linens** you never use

### Bathroom

- **Expired medications** (take to a pharmacy for proper disposal)
- **Old towels and bath mats**
- **Unused toiletries and products**
- **Broken or outdated fixtures** if you are renovating

### Basement and Storage Areas

This is usually where the big stuff lives:

- **Old exercise equipment** gathering dust
- **Broken holiday decorations**
- **Paint cans and leftover renovation materials**
- **Old luggage and bags**
- **Boxes of items from your last move** that you never unpacked

### Garage

- **Flat tires and old car parts**
- **Broken power tools and garden equipment**
- **Leftover building materials** from past projects
- **Old bikes** the kids outgrew
- **Rusty lawn furniture**

### Outdoor Areas

- **Dead plants and garden waste** from last season
- **Broken fencing sections**
- **Old sheds** or playsets past their prime
- **Branches and storm debris** from winter

## What to Do With Everything

### Donate

Items in good, usable condition can be donated to local charities. Furniture, clothing, working appliances, and household goods are always needed. Many charities in London and the surrounding area accept drop-offs or offer pickup for larger items.

### Recycle

Separate out recyclable materials — cardboard, metal, electronics, and certain plastics. Check your municipality for recycling guidelines and drop-off locations.

### Dispose

For everything else — broken items, mixed waste, bulky furniture, and construction debris — a junk removal service is the fastest option. We load it, haul it, and sort it so you do not have to make multiple trips to the dump.
