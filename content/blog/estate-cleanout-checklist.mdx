## Estate Cleanout Checklist: A Step-by-Step Guide for Ontario Families

Clearing out a loved one's estate is one of those tasks that nobody truly feels prepared for. Beyond the emotional weight, there is the sheer logistical challenge of sorting through an entire household — decades of belongings, paperwork, furniture, and memories — and deciding what stays, what goes, and how to handle it all responsibly.

Whether you are managing an estate in London, Grand Bend, or elsewhere in Southwestern Ontario, a structured approach makes the process significantly more manageable. This step-by-step checklist will help you navigate each phase of an estate cleanout, from the initial assessment through to the final sweep.

## Before You Begin: Emotional Preparation

Before diving into the practical work, it is important to acknowledge the emotional reality of an estate cleanout. This is not simply a decluttering project — it is a process tied to grief, family history, and significant life transitions.

- **Give yourself permission to take time.** If the timeline allows, do not rush. Working through a loved one's belongings at a sustainable pace reduces burnout and emotional overwhelm.
- **Enlist support.** Whether it is family members, close friends, or a professional estate cleanout team, having help — both physical and emotional — makes an enormous difference.
- **Set boundaries.** Decide in advance how much you can handle in a single session. Working in focused blocks of a few hours with breaks is far more sustainable than marathon sorting days.
- **Accept that it will be imperfect.** You will not make the perfect decision on every single item. That is normal. Give yourself grace throughout the process.

## Step 1: Assess the Property

Before moving or removing anything, take time to walk through the entire property and get a clear picture of what you are working with.

- **Document the contents.** Take photos or video of each room. This serves as a reference if family members who cannot be present want input, and it may be useful for insurance or legal purposes.
- **Check for hazards.** Look for signs of mould, pest infestations, structural damage, or hazardous materials like old paint, chemicals, or asbestos-containing materials. Address safety concerns before beginning any hands-on work.
- **Identify high-value items early.** Antiques, art, jewellery, collections, and other potentially valuable items should be flagged for appraisal before anything is removed from the property.
- **Review the will and any specific bequests.** Before distributing or removing anything, ensure you understand what the deceased's wishes were regarding specific items.

## Step 2: Handle Important Documents and Valuables First

Estate cleanouts inevitably uncover important paperwork and valuables tucked into unexpected places. Make this your first sorting priority.

### Documents to look for:

- Wills, trusts, and power of attorney paperwork
- Insurance policies (life, home, vehicle)
- Financial records, bank statements, and investment documents
- Property deeds and vehicle titles
- Tax returns and government correspondence
- Birth certificates, marriage certificates, and passports
- Medical records and prescription information

### Where to look:

- Filing cabinets and desk drawers (obvious, but thorough searching is essential)
- Inside books, envelopes, and shoeboxes
- Behind picture frames and inside furniture drawers
- Safe deposit boxes (check with the estate's bank)
- Taped under drawers or inside closets

**Set aside a secure box specifically for documents and valuables.** Do not discard any paperwork until the estate executor or legal counsel has reviewed it.

## Step 3: Sort Everything Into Categories

This is the most time-consuming step, but it is the backbone of an organized cleanout. Work room by room and sort every item into one of four categories:

### Keep
Items with sentimental value, items bequeathed to specific family members, and anything the family collectively wants to preserve. Be selective — keeping too much defeats the purpose and simply relocates the clutter.

### Donate
Furniture, clothing, kitchenware, books, and household items in good condition can be donated to local charities. In the London and Grand Bend area, options include Habitat for Humanity ReStore, the Salvation Army, Goodwill, and various community organisations. Many will arrange pickup for larger items.

### Sell
Antiques, collectables, quality furniture, and specialty items may have meaningful resale value. Options include:

- **Estate sale companies** who manage on-site sales for a percentage of proceeds
- **Online platforms** such as Kijiji, Facebook Marketplace, or specialty auction sites
- **Consignment shops** for quality furniture and decorative items
- **Professional appraisals** for items you suspect may be particularly valuable (fine art, rare books, vintage jewellery, antique furniture)

### Remove
Anything that is broken, worn out, unsaleable, and unsuitable for donation goes into the removal category. This typically includes damaged furniture, outdated electronics, mattresses, expired household products, and general accumulated clutter.

## Step 4: Arrange Appraisals for Valuables

If the estate contains items that may be of significant value — antiques, art, jewellery, coin or stamp collections, vintage tools, or rare books — arrange professional appraisals before selling or distributing them.

- **Contact a certified appraiser** for high-value or specialty items. The Canadian Personal Property Appraiser Group (CPPAG) maintains a directory of qualified professionals.
- **Get multiple opinions** for particularly valuable items.
- **Document appraisals in writing** for estate records and potential tax implications.

Do not assume something is worthless based on appearance alone. Equally, do not assume something is priceless because it is old. A professional assessment protects the estate and all beneficiaries.

## Step 5: Coordinate Donations and Sales

Once items are sorted, move the donation and sale categories out efficiently:

- **Schedule charity pickups** or arrange drop-offs. Book these early, as some organisations have wait times of a week or more.
- **Host an estate sale** if there is enough sellable inventory to justify one. Estate sale companies typically need two to four weeks of lead time.
- **List high-value items online** with clear photographs and honest descriptions.
- **Set a deadline for sales.** Items that do not sell within a reasonable timeframe should be redirected to donation or removal to keep the project moving forward.

## Step 7: Final Cleanup and Property Preparation

Once everything has been removed, the final step is preparing the property for whatever comes next:

- **Deep clean all rooms**, including floors, walls, windows, and fixtures
- **Address any repairs** identified during the initial assessment
- **Arrange lawn care or exterior maintenance** if the property has been unoccupied
- **Cancel or transfer utilities and services** associated with the property
- **Change locks** if the property will be sold or rented
- **Confirm with your real estate agent or lawyer** that the property is ready for the next stage

## Recommended Timeline

Estate cleanouts rarely happen overnight, and forcing the process creates unnecessary stress. Here is a realistic timeline for a standard estate:

- **Weeks 1–2:** Assessment, document retrieval, and initial family discussions about keepsakes and bequests
- **Weeks 2–4:** Room-by-room sorting into keep, donate, sell, and remove categories
- **Weeks 3–5:** Appraisals, estate sales, online listings, and donation coordination
- **Weeks 5–6:** Professional removal of remaining items and final property cleanup

Larger or more complex estates may require additional time. Properties with hoarding conditions, hazardous materials, or legal complications will naturally take longer.
